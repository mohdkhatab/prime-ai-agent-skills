## Ask First
Confirm before drafting: (1) blind pre-NDA or named post-NDA mode; (2) buyer universe and investment type; (3) LTM, current calendar year, or both; (4) 3–5 lead themes; (5) CIM inputs, financial Excel, and 5-year management forecast; and (6) QoE-defined, management-adjusted, or reported EBITDA.

---

## Workflow
1. **Complete the intake gate.** First response asks only the six items above; do not produce deck content before they are answered.
2. **Ingest and classify.** Load the financial workbook/model, select the vertical reference set, and calculate supported growth, margins, CAGRs, and volume metrics. Mark missing forecast years `[TO CONFIRM — pending management model]`; do not extrapolate.
3. **Build the narrative.** Map strengths to named highlight types and select the lead narrative under the buyer/investment defaults.
4. **Build a flexible module map.** Combine, omit, split, or reorder the modules below based on evidence, deal type, and template; do not create placeholder-only slides.
5. **Run the checks** before handing content to the brand/PPTX workflow.

### Financial and Narrative Presentation

The company snapshot must show revenue with growth, LTM revenue, LTM Adj. EBITDA with margin, and at least one vertical-relevant volume metric. Financial performance uses revenue and Adj. EBITDA bars with a margin-line overlay, supported historical/forecast periods, and labeled CAGR(s). Apply one decimal place across financial figures and percentages; render growth and margin percentages in italics; keep bullets to two lines.

Use a standalone narrative for a dual-track process. Sponsor-relevant points include recurring revenue, cash conversion, FCF and leverage capacity; strategic synergy is only a supporting hook. Do not create a slide merely to meet a module map: merge sparse content, surface material gaps in the most relevant module, and retain source lines in all chart/exhibit footers.


The opening narrative is a decision sequence, not a data dump: establish the company profile and reliable headline metrics, then show the market/business evidence, operating proof points, financial trajectory, and the investment highlights those facts support. Market, product, sales/customer, operations, and growth content can share a slide when their relationship is clearer together. If management detail is material, it is a named-mode module; in blind mode, the story relies on anonymized operating and financial evidence instead.

| Vertical | Headline KPIs | Lead volume metrics | Preferred themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS / Software | ARR, NRR, Rule of 40, gross margin | Seats, logos, expansion revenue | Recurring revenue, retention, profitability |
| Industrial / Manufacturing | Revenue, Adj. EBITDA margin, backlog | Backlog, aftermarket mix, utilization | Installed base, annuity, visibility |
| Real Estate / PropCo | NOI, occupancy, WAULT | Sq ft, units, properties | Lease stability, tenancy, pipeline |
| Healthcare Services | Revenue, Adj. EBITDA, same-store growth | Locations, providers, patient volumes | Reimbursement, platform, acuity |
| Consumer / Retail | Revenue, Adj. EBITDA, SSS | Stores, SKUs, loyalty members | Brand, omnichannel, unit economics |
| Business Services | Revenue, ARR/contracted %, EBITDA | Clients, FTEs, retention | Contracts, cross-sell, scalability |


Terminology follows the relevant vertical: SaaS uses ARR/NRR/Rule of 40/CAC/LTV; industrial uses backlog, aftermarket, and capacity utilization; real estate NOI/cap rate/WAULT; healthcare same-store/payor mix/census; consumer SSS/AUV/four-wall EBITDA; business services retention/contracted revenue/NPS.

Use the most favorable accurate volume metric; omit a weak one.

## Rules & Pitfalls

**Never:** include valuation, asking price, implied EV, or transaction multiples; describe a period as improving unless true in the displayed period; use unsupported best-in-class language; or allow rounding drift across slides.

**Conditional:**
- With negative/near-zero EBITDA, lead with revenue, gross profit/margin, ARR/NRR, Rule of 40, or path to breakeven.
- With distress signals, name the remediation and forward trajectory rather than burying the trough.
- If QoE is unavailable, confirm common adjustment items before using management-adjusted EBITDA.
- In blind mode, remove logos/images and identity signals; generalize distinctive geographies, facilities, and customers without blurring the investment story.
- In a dual buyer process, keep synergy to one light hook per relevant page.
- If TAM lacks 2–3 high-fidelity sources, use qualitative language or label an estimate and footnote its source; do not state an unsupported precise market size.
- Forecast gaps use `[TO CONFIRM — pending management model]`; historical-only presentation is required until the model is provided.
- Management appears only in named mode. In blind mode, remove names and generalize references to a seasoned management team.

**Judgment:** Choose a favorable but representative KPI/CAGR period; sell-side framing must remain accurate.


- **Period framing:** The favorable period must still reflect the current business trajectory; do not select an isolated high-growth year merely because it reads well.
- **Distress framing:** A weak operating history cannot support “best-in-class” positioning. Where an inflection exists, identify the cost action, restructuring, refinancing, or management change that supports the trajectory.
- **Management-adjusted EBITDA:** Confirm common add-backs (including one-time legal, M&A transaction, and management add-backs) before use; otherwise label the metric reported rather than Adj. EBITDA.
- **Identity controls:** Blind mode removes not only names, but brand-matching colors, company images/logos, named customers, unique facility counts, and distinctive exact geographies. It should retain sufficient KPI substance for investment evaluation.

## Pre-Delivery Checks
- Module coverage is supported and non-duplicative; repeated figures tie exactly with no rounding drift.
- TAM is sourced to the threshold or qualitative/`(est.)`; forecasts tie to the model or are `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- Naming mode, EBITDA definition, and financial formatting are consistent; blind mode has no identity signals.
- Snapshot has revenue/growth, LTM revenue, LTM Adj. EBITDA/margin, and a volume metric; financials have revenue/EBITDA bars, margin overlay, periods, and CAGRs.
- Highlights have 5–8 discrete supported bullets; no valuation/pricing language appears; every exhibit has a footer source and abbreviations are defined on first use.
- Negative/near-zero EBITDA companies do not headline EBITDA; distressed situations explicitly state the inflection point and no unsupported best-in-class language appears.
- Named mode shows management names/titles; blind mode omits the management module and uses generic leadership references only.
- Dual-track synergy is subordinate to the standalone story, and a weak volume KPI is omitted rather than promoted.
- All financial figures and percentages use one decimal; growth/margin rates are italic and bullets are within two lines.
- Management, market, products, customer, operations, growth, and finance modules are included, combined, omitted, or reordered only on an evidence-based basis—not as page-count padding.
