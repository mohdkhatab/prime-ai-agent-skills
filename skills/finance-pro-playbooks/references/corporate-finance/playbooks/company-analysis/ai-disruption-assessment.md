# AI Disruption Assessment

> **Sections**: Ask First · Workflow · Defaults · Rules & Pitfalls · Pre-Delivery Checks · Scope Boundaries · Task-specific verification
> **Must not skip**: Defaults · Rules & Pitfalls · Pre-Delivery Checks · Scope Boundaries — return here before delivery.

> Task `ai-disruption-assessment` in group `company-analysis`. Read [`_overview.md`](_overview.md) first for group-level scope, shared defaults, and format dependencies.

**Use when**: Structuring an investment judgment on whether AI will disrupt a target company into a scoreable, IC-ready framework | **Deliverable**: Markdown scorecard + .docx scorecard + adaptable .pptx IC deck (typically 3 slides when no page count or template is specified)

---

## Ask First
1. What is the target company's primary business model — services-led (labour leverage) or software-led (system-of-record / data moat)?
2. Does the company operate multiple materially distinct business units (e.g., a TPA with both software and services segments)? If so, provide a revenue breakdown by unit.
3. What is the pricing model — outcome-based / premium / regulated revenue vs. seat-based / time-and-materials?
4. What is the intended holding period, and is there a specific investment decision (underwrite / pass / monitor) this assessment must inform?

> **If only a company name and sector are available:** Ask one focused clarifying question before scoring. Do not fabricate six-dimension scores from insufficient inputs.

---

## Workflow

1. **Collect inputs** — Gather company description, business model, pricing, competitive landscape, and available data assets, regulatory context, and AI disclosures. Score available evidence; reduce confidence and flag supplementary-data gaps in the watch-list.

2. **Score all six dimensions independently on a 1–5 scale** (5 = strong moat / favourable; 1 = weak / exposed) — see dimension definitions below. Attach 1–2 lines of evidence per dimension. If evidence is mixed, assign 3; never force a 2.5 to take a side. Never assign 1 or 5 without explicit supporting evidence.

3. **Compute default composite scores** using the formula below. If the user explicitly specifies different weights or dimensions, calculate those reproducibly as **user-defined scores**, disclose the formula, and do not label them as the Playbook's default scores:

   ```
   Disruption Risk  = avg(6−D1, 6−D2, 6−D3)   // higher = more at risk
   Defensive Power  = avg(D4, D5, D6)            // higher = stronger defence
   ```

   Raw 1–5 dimension scores remain directionally consistent; invert only in the formula selected for the labeled score. When user-defined dimensions or scale differ from the 1–5 default, do not apply the default 2×2 quadrant thresholds without recalibration.

4. **Plot the 2×2 quadrant** — x-axis: Disruption Risk (1–5); y-axis: Defensive Power (1–5). Apply the matrix below. Within ~0.3 of a boundary, flag the point as borderline and identify the watch-list signal for each adjacent quadrant.

5. **Separate multi-segment companies** — Score each material unit across all six dimensions, plot each on its own 2×2, then derive and show a revenue-weighted composite alongside the unit plots.

6. **Write underwriting implications** — Quadrant-specific and company-specific; do not paste generic templates. See quadrant guidance in the Defaults section.

7. **Write the migration thesis** — For every quadrant, answer: *"What must be believed, and what dated evidence must appear within the holding period, for this company to move to an adjacent quadrant?"* Axis movement rules: x-axis (risk) migration requires a change in at least one of D1–D3; y-axis (defensive power) migration requires a change in at least one of D4–D6. See migration rules in Rules & Pitfalls.

8. **Compile watch-list** — 4–6 items, each formatted as: *[Named catalyst] by [date/fiscal period] → [specific dimension] re-scored from [old score] to [new score].* Generic competitive alerts are not acceptable.

9. **Assemble deliverables** — Produce the Markdown scorecard, then render .docx and .pptx per the requested format, page count, or template.

### Six Dimension Definitions

| Code | Dimension | What to Score |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Automation Resistance | Degree to which the work requires judgment that current LLMs cannot replicate — score the **depth of judgment produced**, not the structure of the output format |
| D2 | Disintermediation Resistance | Switching cost and workflow stickiness; ability to be bypassed by a general-purpose LLM or AI-native entrant |
| D3 | Pricing Model Durability | Outcome-based / premium / regulated revenue (durable) vs. seat-based / time-and-materials (compressible by AI) |
| D4 | Proprietary Data & Context | Exclusive longitudinal data generated by customers inside the product vs. publicly purchasable data |
| D5 | Regulatory & Independence Moat | Licences, fiduciary obligations, mandated independence requirements |
| D6 | AI Tailwind Capture | Whether the company itself converts AI into margin expansion, capacity growth, or new SKUs |

### Quadrant Labels

| | **Defensive Power High (≥3)** | **Defensive Power Low (<3)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Disruption Risk Low (<3)** | **Fortress** | **Niche** |
| **Disruption Risk High (≥3)** | **Battleground** | **Vulnerable** |

### Underwriting Implications by Quadrant

| Quadrant | Underwriting Stance |
|---|---|
| **Fortress** | Underwrite multiple expansion + AI-driven margin/capacity upside. Stress-test which specific pillars, if lost, would break the thesis. |
| **Battleground** | Assess whether moat build-out is outpacing commoditisation. Run sensitivity on exit multiple compression. |
| **Niche** | Underwrite stability and execution. Do **not** pay for an AI growth story — the scores do not support it. |
| **Vulnerable** | Require either a credible moat-building roadmap with explicit milestones, or underwrite on commoditisation assumptions (multiple and margin compression). If neither is present, pass. |

### Deliverable Specifications

**.docx Scorecard** must contain in order:
- Cover: company name, quadrant label, date
- Two composite scores with confidence levels
- Six-dimension table: dimension name | raw score | evidence (1–2 lines) | confidence flag if evidence is weak
- Underwriting implications (quadrant-specific and company-specific)
- 4–6 watch-list items (named catalyst / date / dimension / score change)
- Confidence summary and information gaps

**Suggested .pptx IC Deck Structure** *(often 3 slides when no template or page count is specified)*:
- Opening slide: Risk-framing 2×2 with the target plotted as an annotated point (and unit-level points if multi-segment)
- Detail slide(s): Cover all six dimensions with dimension name + score chip (red→green colour scale) + anchor prompt, evidence, and rationale; visually group by composite score
- Combine or split detail slides to preserve legibility and follow the user's requested page count or template.

**Visual system** (apply consistently):
- Navigation-blue section headers
- Red→green risk colour scale: 1 = red, 2 = orange-red, 3 = amber, 4 = light green, 5 = green
- Battleground quadrant: gold highlight
- All score chips use the same colour scale across both decks

## Defaults

**Apply defaults only where the caller has not selected a format or supplied a required assumption; disclose them at delivery.**

| Parameter | Default |
|---|---|
| Scale direction | 5 = strong/favourable; 1 = weak/exposed; never reverse raw scores |
| Uncertain / mixed-evidence score | 3 |
| Extreme score threshold | 1 or 5 requires explicit named evidence |
| Evidence requirement | 1–2 lines per dimension |
| Confidence flag | Use when only industry intuition supports a score |
| Incomplete inputs | Score available evidence; reduce confidence and name gaps in watch-list |
| Borderline boundary | Flag and explain within ~0.3 of a quadrant boundary |
| .pptx length | Requested page count/template; otherwise ~3 slides, minimum readable length retaining quadrant, dimensions, evidence, and material gaps |
| Applicable sectors | Technology and white-collar services: SaaS, data/information, professional services, insurance/brokerage, fintech |

## Rules & Pitfalls

**Never:**
- Never score output **format** as D1; score required judgment depth. Structured banking, legal, and clinical outputs can require non-replicable judgment and generally score above 2 on D1.
- Never show a multi-segment composite without unit scores and 2×2 plots.
- Never assign 1 or 5 without named, explicit evidence.
- Never use a generic watch-list alert; name catalyst, date/fiscal period, dimension, and score direction.
- Never use boilerplate underwriting across quadrants; make it quadrant- and company-specific.
- Never apply this framework to physical/heavy industry; use an AI-augmentation lens instead.

**Conditional:**
- Mixed services/software business: apply the services lens (labour leverage, AI productivity) and software lens (system of record, data moat, replicability) separately.
- Within ~0.3 of a boundary: include a borderline paragraph and the trigger for each adjacent quadrant.
- Name and sector only: ask one focused clarification; do not construct six-dimension scores.

**Judgment:**
- Treat a diagonal migration (e.g., Vulnerable → Fortress) as a base-case red flag. Decompose it through Battleground or Niche, with dated evidence for each step.
- Score mixed services and software through their respective lenses rather than blending them.
- Where evidence cannot differentiate directionally, use 3 rather than force 2 or 4.

## Pre-Delivery Checks

- For the default six-dimension method, raw scores are 1–5 and use `Disruption Risk = avg(6−D1, 6−D2, 6−D3)` and `Defensive Power = avg(D4, D5, D6)`. For a user-requested variant, verify the supplied dimensions, weights, calculation, scale and user-defined label instead of forcing the default formula.
- Scores of 1/5 have named evidence; every dimension has 1–2 evidence lines or a low-confidence flag.
- Boundary-near points have a borderline explanation; multi-segment companies show each unit plus the revenue-weighted composite.
- Every watch-list item names catalyst, date/fiscal period, dimension, and score direction; implications are quadrant- and company-specific.
- Migration has dated evidence triggers; a diagonal move is decomposed through an intermediate quadrant.
- Visual system is consistent: navigation-blue headers, 1=red through 5=green, Battleground gold.
- .pptx contains quadrant, six dimensions, evidence, and material gaps at the requested length/template or minimum readable length.

## Scope Boundaries

Quantitative valuation (DCF, 3-statement modelling, LBO, comps) is handled by the financial modelling playbooks — this playbook produces qualitative structured scoring that informs underwriting stance, not a standalone valuation. Physical and heavy-industry companies fall outside this framework's applicable scope; AI-augmentation analysis for those sectors requires a separate playbook. Credit memo drafting and full IC memo composition are handled by their respective writing playbooks; this playbook produces the AI risk scorecard section and IC deck insert only.

## Task-specific verification

Weight each dimension by same-period, same-currency material-unit revenue: w_i=revenue_i/Σcovered revenue. Missing material revenue or incompatible units makes the company composite provisional. Classify using unrounded values; display two decimals; borderline distance is ≤0.30 from the relevant threshold.
